Susan Collins asks for a sixth chance

By Jada Shannon

October 21, 2026

 This November, Maine's longest-serving Senator Susan Collins is running for re-election against Democrat Troy Jackson. If Collins wins, she would serve her sixth term as Senator, continuing a decade-long winning streak that began when she first took office in 1997. At 73, Collins is now the longest-serving Republican woman in the Senate.

Affordability

 Only 54% of Maine residents are meeting their basic needs, according to a national study from the Brookings Institution. Affordability is a major concern in Maine, with residents expressing concerns about the cost of food, gas, healthcare, and housing. On affordability, Collins flexes her advocacy for reducing prescription drug prices. In a recent interview on the podcast “All Things with Kim Strassel,” Collins discusses her bill that would cap insulin prices for private insurers if passed. However, her platform page neglects to cover other areas of concern.

Healthcare

 Susan Collins does not support Medicare for All, the leading policy proposal from the progressive wing of the Democratic Party. Rather, she points to her leadership in founding the Senate Diabetes Caucus and tripling federal funding for diabetes research. She mentions her role as co-chair of the task force on Alzheimer’s disease, where she also advocates for research funding.

Housing

 In Maine, the cost of buying or renting a home has reached record highs. On the issue of housing, Collins supported the “Road to Housing Act,” which passed the Senate with bipartisan support. If the bill passes the House, it would speed up the review process for project proposals and limit the power of investors to bulk-buy single-family homes.

Trump

 On President Trump, Collins has a pattern of aligning herself with his interests while disavowing his actions in public. In the final live debate between Collins and Democrat Troy Jackson, Collins was asked to name an instance of voting against Trump and the Republican Party to meaningfully change the outcome of an initiative. In response, she struggled to name a consequential vote and prove her ability to stand up to President Trump.
